Sabudana Khichdi Recipe

How to make Sabudana Khichdi (Stepwise)

To make Sabudana Khichdi, the first step is always soaking the sabudana well. This helps the pearls turn soft, fluffy, and separate after cooking. If not soaked properly, the khichdi can become sticky or lumpy.

Soak Sabudana

1. Rinse 1 cup sabudana, sago or tapioca pearls a couple of times in running water until all the starch is washed away. You can use a colander while rinsing.
Soak sabudana in water. The water level can be 1.5 to 2 inches above the sabudana.
In the picture below, sabudana pearls are already soaked. Depending on the quality of sabudana, you can soak them overnight or for some hours.
I always soak them overnight, since even after soaking them for 2 to 3 hours, they don’t soften.

2. To check if sabudana has become soft or not, you should be able to easily press the sabudana and it should get mashed easily. If there is some hardness in the center of the sago pearls, then add a few tablespoons of water in the bowl. Cover and leave for 30 minutes more.

3. Using a strainer or colander, strain all the water from sabudana. Strain very well as there should be no extra water in the sabudana. Keep aside. Remember to drain the water really very well. Any water logged in the sago pearls will make your khichdi mushy or pasty.

4. While sabudana is getting soaked, you can boil 2 medium-sized potatoes in a pan or in a cooker and keep aside. For pressure cooking, just add enough water covering the potatoes and then pressure cook on medium heat for 4 to 5 whistles. You can also steam the potatoes in an Instant Pot, adding required amount of water. Note that I usually steam or boil the potatoes, but you could opt to shallow fry small cubes of potato in some oil until light golden and crispy.

5. When the pressure settles down naturally in the cooker, then only open the lid. Remove the potatoes and let them become warm or cool completely. Then, peel and chop them.

6. Heat a kadai (wok) or a frying pan. Add ½ cup peanuts (moongphali). Keep heat to medium-low and stirring often, roast the peanuts.
7. Roast till the peanuts become crunchy. You will see the peanut skins getting browned or charred. 8. Turn off the heat and keep the peanuts aside to cool. You can remove the peanut skins, if you want. 9. Add the cooled roasted peanuts in a small grinder jar or blender.

10. Using the pulse option, powder the peanuts coarsely. Do not grind at a stretch, as then the peanuts will release their fat.
Grind in parts and for a few seconds as we just need to make a coarse or semi-fine peanut powder. You can even coarsely powder the peanuts in a mortar-pestle.
11. Take the well drained and soaked sago pearls in a mixing bowl, plate or tray. Then, add the ground peanut powder in to it.
12. Also add edible rock salt (sendha namak) as per taste and ½ to 1 teaspoon sugar.
In the Hindu fasting food, we use rock salt. If making this dish on regular or non-fasting days, then you can swap rock salt with regular salt.

13. Mix very well with a spoon.

14. Heat 3 tablespoons peanut oil or ghee (or your preferred oil) in a heavy kadai or pan. Keep the heat to a low or medium-low.
15. Add 1 teaspoon cumin seeds. Let them crackle and get browned.
16. Next, add 1 chopped green chilli (about ½ to 1 teaspoon, chopped).
I do not add curry leaves to the sabudana khichdi that I make during the fasting or vrat.
But accordingly as followed in your family, if curry leaves are allowed as a fasting ingredient, you can choose to add them.

17. Fry on medium-low heat for a few secon
18. Now, add the chopped boiled potatoes.
19. Mix and sauté on medium-low heat for a minute.

20. Next, add the sabudana-peanut powder mixture.
21. Sauté on a low heat, stirring continuously, for about 3 to 5 minutes.
